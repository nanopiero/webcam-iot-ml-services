Section 1 — Acquisition, storage, queueing, orchestration, and initial observability

Objective
Run the acquisition path on EWC: receive notifications, maintain stream
metadata, archive images and sidecars, prepare NFS processing inputs, and
publish Kafka jobs. No inference consumer runs during this phase. The resulting
backlog is intentional and will be consumed in Phase 2 catch-up mode.

Workload baseline
The fleet contains approximately 25,000 webcams, but produces approximately
10,000 new images per five minutes, not 25,000 images every five minutes.
The nominal mean arrival rate is therefore about 33 new images per second.
Retain the architecture's burst scenario of 10,000 arrivals over one minute
(about 167 images/second), and assess 200 images/second as its margin target.
Measure processing jobs separately from source images: panorama splitting
increases job counts, while status and solar-phase rules reduce eligibility.
Derive storage projections from measured image sizes, arrival rates, sidecars,
and eligibility; fleet size alone does not determine retained volume.

WP1.1 — Configuration, contracts, and initial database
Dependencies: none.
Tasks:
- Establish and document service package boundaries, dependency direction,
  transaction boundaries, diagnostic entry points, and unit/integration test
  placement before extending the live Acquisition path.
- Define deployment configuration for MQTT, spool/archive S3, NFS, PostgreSQL,
  Kafka, stream-generation rules, worker concurrency, and operation timeouts.
- Capture representative upstream notifications and document the fields used
  for image identity, timestamps, image signatures, metadata, and retrieval.
- Create the initial derived_stream and processing_stream schema on weow-db,
  including identifiers, profile metadata, flags, slice intervals, and stored
  partition assignments required by Acquisition.
- Specify dictionary initialization and synchronized metadata updates, with
  deterministic stream/profile resolution and stable partition allocation.
Deliverables: code-organization note, configuration template, schema/migrations,
notification fixtures, and acquisition data-contract notes.
Completion: existing and newly discovered streams can be resolved consistently,
including concurrent discovery, metadata changes, and recurring profiles.

WP1.2 — Archive, NFS, and Kafka foundations
Dependencies: WP1.1 contracts and configuration.
Tasks:
- Configure durable image/sidecar storage and spool access.
- Deploy the shared NFS on weow-a with the planned processing-image and initial
  state layout; verify visibility from a client representing a processing VM.
- Deploy the pilot Kafka broker on weow-a and operational topic with 50
  partitions, explicit retention configuration, and persistent broker storage.
- Verify successful archive and complete NFS publication before job emission.
- Estimate available NFS and Kafka capacity against the planned acquisition-only
  duration; configure capacity alerts and a stop condition before exhaustion.
Deliverables: reproducible service configuration and storage-access checks.
Completion: complete archived images, sidecars, NFS inputs, and acknowledged
Kafka messages can be read back; configured capacity supports the initial run.

WP1.3 — Acquisition implementation
Dependencies: WP1.1; WP1.2 for integration validation.
Tasks:
- Implement notification handling, metadata dictionaries, stream creation,
  solar-phase evaluation, status filtering, and deterministic panorama slicing.
- Retrieve each eligible archive image once; persist its image and metadata
  sidecar, including resolved processing streams and acquisition timestamp.
- Prepare processing images and initial stream state on NFS, then publish
  one job per eligible processing stream to its assigned Kafka partition.
- Implement bounded worker concurrency and the configured short retry policy.
- Preserve the accepted crash behavior: in-flight work may be lost or resent.
  Do not add durable acquisition recovery or reconciliation. Do not discard
  notifications solely because MQTT msg.dup=1.
Deliverables: runnable Acquisition service and meaningful contract/integration
checks for filtering, slicing, metadata transitions, and publication sequencing.
Completion: representative daytime, transition, nighttime, panoramic, blacklisted,
and greylisted inputs produce the expected archives, streams, and jobs.

WP1.4 — Orchestration and initial observability
Dependencies: WP1.2; WP1.3 service interfaces.
Tasks:
- Deploy Prometheus and initial dashboards for service and infrastructure data.
- Expose notification rates, filtering outcomes, stage durations/failures,
  archived image counts/bytes, job publication rates, and metadata update failures.
- Monitor worker queue depth/age, memory, CPU, network, NFS occupancy, database
  performance, and Kafka log growth. Distinguish intentional Kafka accumulation
  without consumers from an acquisition bottleneck.
- Define the Kestra Acquisition workflow for launch, supervision, failure
  relaunch, and daily container restart with dictionary reconstruction.
Deliverables: workflow configuration, dashboards, and capacity/failure alerts.
Completion: a controlled stop/restart is visible and service resumes; observed
crash losses or resends are reported without claiming acquisition recovery.

WP1.5 — Benchmark preparation and acquisition performance validation
Dependencies: prepare alongside WP1.1; execute after WP1.2–WP1.4 integration.
Tasks:
- Build a repeatable workload driver using representative notification/image
  fixtures and a test spool. Include actual size/aspect-ratio distributions,
  panoramic inputs, stream-rate variation, and operational eligibility ratios.
- Include both steady metadata and a discovery/metadata-change burst, so the
  database and dictionary update paths are exercised at realistic load.
- Use isolated benchmark object prefixes, stream identities, and Kafka topic
  with production-equivalent settings to keep synthetic jobs out of live history.
- Exercise nominal load (10,000 new images/five minutes), the one-minute burst,
  and the 200-image/second margin target. Record input images and resulting jobs
  separately. Specify test duration and warm-up in each reproducible run recipe.
- Measure notification receipt to Kafka acknowledgement and each stage's
  p50/p95/p99 latency; retain upstream download/publication timestamps to expose
  delays before Acquisition. Measure throughput, errors, queue depth/age, and
  burst drain time as well as CPU, memory, network, and storage usage.
- Repeat representative runs at different times of day to characterize EWC
  variability. Compare S3 and NFS operation rates and latency, not just MB/s.
- Project archive, sidecar, NFS, and Kafka growth from measurements, including
  the acquisition-only backlog and planned retention. Document headroom.
- Produce a benchmark report with workload, deployment/code versions, service
  settings, results, bottlenecks, and configuration changes needed before launch.
Acceptance:
- Nominal load is sustained without growing acquisition queues or unexplained
  losses in failure-free runs; burst queues drain after arrivals return to normal.
- Check whether Acquisition can sustain the 200-image/second margin target;
  record any shortfall and resolve or explicitly revise sizing before scaling up.
- Allocate and record an acquisition latency budget within the overall five-minute
  service target. Meeting an acquisition budget alone does not establish the
  complete service's end-to-end latency or inference capacity.
- No storage exhaustion occurs within the planned acquisition-only interval.
Deliverables: workload fixtures/driver, run recipes, metrics, and benchmark report.

Accepted validation choice (2026-09-18): use the real `webcam/T0` feed and
real ingestion-spool objects rather than synthetic replay for the closing
capacity result. The accepted 15-minute, 24-worker run kept a zero worker queue
through observed bursts above 40 images/second. Defer longer traffic-variation
coverage to a one-week endurance run. That run requires active consumers or
additional NFS capacity because the present NFS holds about three nominal days
of processing images. See `benchmarks/results/wp15_live_002.md`.

WP1.6 — Controlled launch and Phase 2 handover
Dependencies: WP1.1–WP1.5 complete.
Tasks:
- Launch Acquisition at a controlled scope, then increase to the pilot workload
  using measured throughput and capacity headroom.
- Validate archived image/sidecar correspondence, NFS processing inputs, job
  metadata, partition distribution, and stream database updates.
- Record acquisition start time, topic/partition configuration, backlog volume
  and age, resource usage, and available capacity for Phase 2 catch-up.
- Keep required NFS inputs and initial states available for catch-up; do not
  introduce age-only deletion while consumers/checkpoints do not yet exist.
Deliverables: Phase 1 validation record and operational handover notes.
Completion: Acquisition runs at the agreed workload with observable, bounded
resource growth and a documented backlog ready for processing.

Benchmark follow-through in later sections
- Phase 2: benchmark full processing with Triton, state persistence, database
  writes, catch-up, and one-consumer operation; validate complete service latency
  once publication is connected.
- Phase 3: include E-SOH transmission and observation availability in the
  end-to-end latency assessment.
- Phase 4: benchmark scene-history rebuilding, backfill, C+Q, and maintenance
  duration against the available execution window and operational handover.
These later work packages will be detailed with their respective sections.
